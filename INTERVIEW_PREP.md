## Walkthrough: Zeneith (2 minutes)
- Context: an existing Active Enrollments table had many columns and several insurance concepts competing for attention.
- Goal: make renewal information scannable and offer a mobile-friendly alternative.
- Choices: prioritize provider names and renewal dates, separate policies visually, use a vertical reading flow on mobile.
- Tradeoff: the concept does not display every original administrative action or status; explain how you'd preserve these in a full workflow rather than claiming they are solved.
- Next steps: interview target users, map their primary tasks, test findability of renewal dates and plan status, run accessibility checks, and iterate.

## Walkthrough: Cloudhire (90 seconds)
- Problem: talent discovery could be approached through multiple employer mental models.
- Choice: provide three discovery groups—skills, roles, and careers—and use a consistent tile pattern.
- Tradeoff: small type and generic expansion labels should be corrected; clicking a category requires a clearly designed next step.
- Next steps: test whether employers find a relevant specialty and understand filter/search results.

## Likely questions and strong answer directions
1. Why did you change the layout? Discuss the user's task and reading order, not aesthetics alone.
2. How would you test it? Define a realistic task, observe users, measure task completion/error/confusion, revise.
3. What would you improve in your own work? Mention small text/contrast and completeness of original administrative actions.
4. How do you collaborate with developers? Reference responsive constraints, components, states, HTML/CSS/JS understanding, clear documentation.
5. How would you design for caregivers? Reduce cognitive load, prioritize critical tasks, use plain language, accessible type, confirmation for consequential actions, and respect privacy.
6. What if the COO disagrees with a design? Ask what customer/business outcome concerns them, show alternatives, and propose a lightweight test.
7. Have you conducted user research? Answer truthfully. If not, explain how you would recruit participants and conduct a moderated task test.

## Questions to ask the COO
- Who is the primary user for the first product release: family caregiver, care recipient, or clinician?
- What are the top two tasks users must complete without confusion?
- How are design decisions currently validated with caregivers?
- What does success look like for the junior designer in the first 60–90 days?
- How do design and engineering collaborate on handoff and iteration?
